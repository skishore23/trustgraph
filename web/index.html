Decide where your agent's output can go, based on what it read.

TrustGraph is policy-as-code for what AI agents produce. One small YAML file says who may read each source and who receives each action. Every output gets allow, needs approval or deny, with the reason.

    The problem

    Permission systems decide what an agent may read. They lose track the moment it writes something new.

    1. It reads two allowed files.

      The salary file and the roadmap. Each read passes its permission check.

    2. It writes something new.

      A Q3 headcount plan built from both. A new document has no permissions of its own.

    3. It posts where it shouldn't.

      The plan goes to #engineering, and the salaries go with it.

    TrustGraph makes the restriction follow the data.

    Simulate

    Seeded runs drive the real runtime through policy changes, crashes and approval expiry, and check five safety invariants at every step. They cannot cover what your system does after allow.

    Written by agents, approved by people

    Agents draft the policy: every command has --json output with a stable code, a path and a did-you-mean suggestion. A person reviews the plain-English explanation and a diff that flags every widening.

    $ trustgraph policy check --json trustgraph.policy.yaml

    The rule

    readers(output) = readers(input 1) ∩ readers(input 2) ∩ …
allowed         = everyone the action reaches ⊆ readers(output)

    Approval adds a human check; it never widens who may read a source. Anything the policy does not name is denied.

    How we know it works

    Deterministic

    No language model in the decision path. Same policy, same inputs, same answer.

    Repeatable is not right: your tests say what right means.

    Simulated

    The simulator runs the shipped runtime and checks five invariants after every step.

    Evidence, not a proof over every execution.

    Proved, within bounds

    Ten Kani proofs cover the decision ordering, the approval window and the reader rules. All pass in CI.

    Those kernels within fixed bounds, not the whole system.

    Use it

    TrustGraph ships as a CLI and Rust crates.

    cargo install --locked --git https://github.com/skishore23/trustgraph trustgraph-cli

trustgraph policy init --path trustgraph.policy.yaml
trustgraph policy check trustgraph.policy.yaml
trustgraph policy test trustgraph.policy.yaml
trustgraph policy simulate trustgraph.policy.yaml --seed 0
trustgraph policy explain trustgraph.policy.yaml
trustgraph policy diff old.policy.yaml trustgraph.policy.yaml

    To let agents ask before they act, run trustgraph serve --policy trustgraph.policy.yaml --operator leo: a local HTTP and MCP service. Serve guide

    Limits

    • It only knows the inputs your system reports.
    • It decides; it does not act. Ask right before acting, and act only on allow.
    • One sensitive input restricts the whole output.
    • Groups are lists of names in the file, not a directory.

    Where it fits

    Permission databases such as SpiceDB or OpenFGA decide what your agent may read. TrustGraph decides where what it wrote may go.
